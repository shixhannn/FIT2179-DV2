Malaysia Tourism Recovery

Malaysia’s tourism industry experienced major disruption during the COVID-19 pandemic. This dashboard explores how domestic travel and international arrivals recovered over time, and where visitor activity was concentrated across Malaysia.

Introduction

Malaysia’s tourism recovery can be seen through both domestic travel activity and the rebound of international arrivals. The charts below compare seasonal domestic patterns with the wider recovery trend from 2020 onwards.

Domestic travel increased strongly after 2021, with higher visitor numbers across nearly all quarters by 2024 and 2025.

Domestic visitors recovered faster and reached much higher volumes than international arrivals.

Where Malaysians Travelled

Domestic tourism did not recover evenly across all states. These maps show where local visitors travelled most, and how visitor patterns compare with household income levels.

Domestic visitors were concentrated in major states such as Selangor, Perak and Pahang.

Higher-income states do not always receive the highest domestic visitor volumes, showing that tourism demand is shaped by more than income alone.

International Tourism

International arrivals recovered more unevenly after the pandemic. This section shows where Malaysia’s visitors came from, which countries contributed the most arrivals, and where international arrivals concentrated within Malaysia.

Most top international source markets are located within Asia-Pacific, highlighting Malaysia’s dependence on nearby regional travel markets.

Singapore remained the dominant source market, while China, Indonesia and Thailand also became key contributors during recovery.

International arrivals were not evenly distributed across Malaysia, with stronger concentration in selected entry states.

Conclusion

Malaysia’s tourism recovery shows two connected patterns: domestic travel recovered strongly across states and quarters, while international arrivals rebounded unevenly, led by nearby source markets such as Singapore, Indonesia, China and Thailand.
