Malaysia on the Move

From empty airports to bustling destinations, Malaysia's tourism recovery was shaped by
changing travel habits, spending patterns and the return of international visitors.
Explore how tourism rebounded across the country after the COVID-19 pandemic.

The Recovery Began at Different Speeds

Domestic travel rebounded strongly from 2022 onwards, while international arrivals took longer
to recover after border restrictions eased. By 2024, domestic visitors reached
a record 211.6 million, far outpacing the international rebound.

Not Every State Shared the Recovery

Tourism activity became concentrated in a handful of states. Selangor, Perak and Pahanh
emerged as dominant hubs, while higher household income did not consistently predict
higher visitor volumes, suggesting that accessibility and attractions matter more than wealth.

Visitors Returned From Nearby Markets First

International tourism recovery was led by neighbouring countries. Singapore alone contributed nearly
half of all international arrivals in 2024, more than four times any other source market.
International visitors also concentrated in a few key entry states, highlighting Malaysia's
dependence on regional connectivity.

Malaysians Travel and Spend Differently Now

Recovery was not only about volume. It reshaped travel behaviour.
Visiting relatives and friends remained the top purpose of travel,
while shopping gained share. Spending rose across every major category
from 2023 to 2024, with shopping remaining the single largest expenditure item.

Conclusion

Malaysia's tourism sector has largely recovered, driven by strong domestic travel and regional international markets. However, tourism activity remains concentrated in selected states and source countries, suggesting that future growth will depend on broadening both visitor distribution and market diversity.
